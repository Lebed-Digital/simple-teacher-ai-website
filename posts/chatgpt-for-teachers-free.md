If you have been paying for ChatGPT out of your own pocket, you can probably stop. OpenAI has a plan called ChatGPT for Teachers, and it is free for U.S. K-12 teachers and school staff **through June 2028**. Most teachers I talk to either have not heard of it or tried to sign up once and got stuck on the verification screen.

Here is who qualifies, what you get, the exact steps, and what to do when verification fails.

*Last checked November 8, 2026, against OpenAI's [ChatGPT for K-12 teachers page](https://chatgpt.com/plans/k12-teachers/) and [help center article](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12844995-chatgpt-for-teachers). These offers change, so check the official page before you rely on a date.*

## Who qualifies

You qualify if you are currently employed by an accredited K-12 school or district in the United States. That is wider than just classroom teachers. OpenAI lists teachers, school staff, administrators, and district leaders.

You do not qualify if you are a student, a college or university instructor, or a teacher outside the U.S.

## What you actually get

According to OpenAI's plan page, ChatGPT for Teachers includes:

- unlimited access to OpenAI's flagship model
- file uploads, so you can hand it a worksheet or a unit plan
- voice mode, image generation, and data analysis
- web search, memory, and deep research
- connectors and apps
- custom GPTs and shared projects, so a grade-level team can share one setup

For most teachers, the part that matters is the usage limit. On a free personal account, you hit a wall right when you are in the middle of batching a week of exit tickets. This plan is built so that does not happen. If you want the longer version of why that matters, I wrote about [what free and paid AI plans actually differ on](/blog/free-vs-paid-ai-tools-for-teachers).

## How to sign up, step by step

1. **Go to the [ChatGPT for K-12 teachers page](https://chatgpt.com/plans/k12-teachers/)** and start the sign-up.
2. **Sign in or create an account with your school or district email.** Not your personal Gmail. This is the step that trips up the most people, because verification checks your school email.
3. **Verify through SheerID.** OpenAI uses a company called SheerID to confirm you work at a school. For a lot of teachers this is instant: it checks official sources or has you sign in through your school's login. If it cannot confirm you that way, it asks you to upload a document showing you currently work there. The upload screen tells you what it accepts.
4. **Create your workspace.** Once you are verified, choose "Create workspace." You can use it on your own or invite other teachers from your school or district. Workspaces are limited to verified staff in the same district.

That is it. If verification went through instantly, the whole thing takes a few minutes.

## When verification gets stuck

This is where most people give up, so here is what to try.

**You signed in with the wrong email.** If you started with a personal email, sign out and start over with your school email. Verify with that same address. Mixing two accounts is the most common reason it fails.

**You get an "Unknown error" when you upload your document.** OpenAI's help center says to open the verification page in a different browser, sign in with the same ChatGPT account and the same sign-in method you used before, and try the upload again.

**It still will not go through.** Contact OpenAI Support through the chat bubble in the [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12844995-chatgpt-for-teachers). If your district already set up a workspace, your tech department may also be able to add you directly.

## What about student privacy?

This is the part I would not skip. OpenAI says what you share in ChatGPT for Teachers is not used to train its models by default, and the data is encrypted. OpenAI also says authorized staff and contractors can review conversations for abuse and legal reasons, so "private" does not mean nobody can ever see it.

So the rule I follow does not change: [take student names and identifying details out before you paste](/blog/ai-privacy-mistake-student-names). Use "Student A." Put the real name back in yourself afterward. Your district's rules decide what is allowed, not the tool's marketing page.

## What happens after June 2028?

OpenAI says pricing may change after June 2028, and that its goal is to keep it affordable and give advance notice. Nothing is decided yet. Sign up now, use it for free, and decide later whether it is worth paying for.
