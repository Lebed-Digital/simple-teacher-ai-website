I wrote a set of directions last year that made perfect sense to me. Then I watched half the class stall on step two. I had put three actions in one sentence, used a word they did not know, and assumed they would remember the thing I had said out loud once.

The wording was the problem. Now I run the directions through AI before I put them on the board.

## The prompt I actually use

```
Rewrite the directions below so they are clear and easy for my students to follow. Use short steps, simple words, and number each step. Keep it appropriate for third grade. Do not add extra steps I did not include. Do not make it cute.

Directions:
[PASTE THE DIRECTIONS HERE]
```

"Do not add extra steps" is the line I used to forget. Without it, I get a whole new procedure, complete with materials I do not have and a turn-and-talk I did not ask for. I already have the activity. I need the wording cleaned up.

I have used this in ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. The tool matters less than those two lines: numbered steps, and do not invent a new task.

A direction I used to write in one breath: "Look at the clocks on the worksheet, compare each one to the time shown, and circle the clock that matches, then write the time on the line."

What I want back is closer to:

```
1. Look at the first clock.
2. Circle the clock that shows the same time.
3. Write that time on the line.
```

I still read it. I still change a word if it is the word we are actually learning. I do not want cute. I want steps I can put on the board without translating them in my head. Sometimes it simplifies past the point of the lesson. If the math word is the point, I put it back.

The first draft is a starting point, the same way a [generic lesson comes back generic](/blog/why-ai-keeps-giving-generic-teacher-stuff) until you tell it what your Tuesday actually looks like.

## What I do not use this for

I do not ask it to write the activity. I already have that from the curriculum, which is the same line I draw with [lesson planning](/blog/is-it-okay-for-teachers-to-use-ai-lesson-plans). Directions are a wording job. The lesson is mine.

I also do not paste a whole curriculum page in. A few sentences in my own words is enough, and it keeps me on the right side of whatever my district says about sharing materials. Same reason I [outline a lesson instead of dumping the whole document](/blog/ai-prompt-find-where-students-will-struggle) when I ask where kids will get stuck.

No student names. This prompt does not need them, and [taking names out](/blog/ai-privacy-mistake-student-names) is still the habit I keep for everything else.

## Try it on tomorrow's independent work

Take the directions you were going to write on the board, paste them in, and read the numbered version out loud. If you would still have to explain it three times, tell it which step is confusing and run it once more.
